1. The Basics
Players: Exactly 3 players.

Deck: A standard 52-card Anglo-American deck (Jokers removed).

Objective: To get rid of all the cards in your hand, or to have the lowest total point value of unmatched cards in your hand when the game ends.

2. Card Values and Rankings
When tallying points at the end of a round, the cards hold the following values:

Ace: 1 point

Cards 2 to 9: Face value (e.g., a 5 is worth 5 points)

10, Jack, Queen, King: 10 points each

3. Valid Melds (Bahay)
To get rid of cards, you must form them into melds. There are two types of valid melds:

Three or Four of a Kind: Three or four cards of the same rank (e.g., 7♠ - 7♥ - 7♣).

Straight Flush: Three or more cards in sequential order of the exact same suit (e.g., 4♥ - 5♥ - 6♥ - 7♥).

4. Setup and Dealing
The dealer is chosen randomly for the first game. In subsequent games, the winner of the previous round becomes the dealer.

The dealer deals 13 cards to themselves and 12 cards to the other two players.

The remaining cards are placed face down in the center to form the Stock Pile.

Because the dealer has 13 cards, they take the first turn automatically by discarding one card face up to start the Discard Pile.

5. Taking a Turn
Play moves counter-clockwise. On your turn, you must complete these actions in order:

Draw or Chow:

Draw: Take the top card from the Stock Pile.

Chow (Pick up): Take the top card from the Discard Pile. You can only do this if you can immediately use that discarded card to form a valid meld with the cards currently in your hand. You must lay that meld down on the table right away.

Drop Melds (Optional): You can place any completed melds from your hand face-up on the table in front of you.

Sapaw / Connect (Optional): You can add cards to any exposed melds already on the table—whether they belong to you or your opponents. For example, if an opponent has dropped 4♥ - 5♥ - 6♥, and you have the 7♥, you can place it on their meld.

Discard: To end your turn, you must discard one card from your hand face up onto the discard pile.

6. How to Win
There are three ways to win a round of Tongits:

Method 1: Tongits
If you successfully meld and/or connect all of your cards, and have one final card left to discard, you win by "Tongits." You have successfully emptied your hand.

Method 2: Calling a "Draw" (The Fight)
If you believe the unmatched cards left in your hand have the lowest point total compared to your opponents, you can call a "Draw" at the very beginning of your turn (before drawing a card).

Conditions to call a Draw: You must have exposed at least one meld on the table, and no opponent can have connected (Sapaw) to any of your melds during the round since your last turn.

The Fight: Once a Draw is called, the other players can either Fold (concede if they know their points are too high) or Fight (challenge the caller).

Whoever has the lowest point total in their hand wins. If there is a tie, the challenger wins over the caller.

Method 3: Deck Exhaustion
If the Stock Pile runs out of cards and no one has won by Tongits or called a Draw, the game immediately ends.

Players tally the points of the unmatched cards remaining in their hands.

The player with the lowest score wins.

7. Crucial Rules and Penalties
Burned (Sunog): If the game ends (either by the deck running out or someone calling a Draw) and you have not dropped a single meld on the table, you are "burned." You automatically lose and usually pay a penalty, regardless of how low your points are.

Secret Melds: If you have four-of-a-kind in your hand, you do not have to expose it to be safe from getting burned, but it counts as zero points. However, if an opponent drops three-of-a-kind and you hold the fourth card, you cannot keep it as a secret meld.

Two-Card Drops (Special House Rule): Some local variations allow you to drop two cards of a straight flush if they are both "heads" (e.g., Ace-2 of Spades) to avoid getting burned, but in strict official rules, a meld must be a minimum of three card
